# Career Philosophy

> **Purpose:** Zentavio's model of what makes a career succeed. Every score, gap, path, and
> transition estimate encodes this model — so it is written down rather than left implicit in
> a scoring function.

## What actually makes a career work

Ranked roughly by how much they move outcomes, and each one is modeled, not just believed:

1. **Transferable skills.** Careers move on what carries over, not on job titles. The skill
   graph exists so transfer is computed from weighted edges instead of keyword overlap.
2. **Continuous learning.** Trajectory beats snapshot. Someone visibly closing gaps is a
   better bet than someone static at a higher level.
3. **Demonstrated work.** A portfolio, shipped systems, contributions — evidence outranks
   claims. Requirements are met by proof, not by self-assessment.
4. **Relevant experience.** Real, but the most over-weighted signal in the market. Years are
   a proxy for skills we can measure directly; when we can measure them, we prefer the
   measurement.
5. **Communication.** Explaining decisions, writing clearly, working across a language
   boundary. Decisive in interviews and in relocation.
6. **Adaptability.** Handling unfamiliar tools and domains. The strongest predictor of a
   successful transition and the hardest to evidence — never asserted without a basis.
7. **Networking and market access.** Referrals and reachability matter, sometimes more than
   fit. Modeled as access, never as a substitute for readiness.

## What we deliberately do not treat as merit

Prestige of employer or school, years-in-seat, title inflation, certification count without
demonstrated skill, and any proxy that correlates with background rather than capability.
These appear in the market and may be recorded as market signal — they never become the basis
of a Zentavio judgment about a person.

## How this shapes the models

**Careers are graphs, not ladders.** A career is a position in a graph with adjacency and
observed transition paths. "What could I do?" is a traversal, not a list.

**Gaps are weighted and ordered.** A skill gap is not a set difference. Each missing skill
carries a weight (how much it matters for the target) and a dependency position (what must
come first). An unordered gap list is not a plan.

**Readiness is honest and has a remainder.** A readiness score always states what is still
missing and how long closing it plausibly takes. A number without a remainder is a vanity
metric.

**Transitions have a cost.** Every transition estimate carries effort, elapsed time, and
plausibility given this person's starting point — not a generic difficulty rating.

**Evidence outranks assertion.** A claimed skill and an evidenced skill are different rows
with different weights. Confidence in the person's profile is as explicit as confidence in a
fact.

**Relocation viability is eligibility × employability.** Visa-eligible and unhirable is not
an option; hirable and ineligible is not an option. Both are computed, and the binding
constraint is named.

## Timelines and honesty

Learning takes as long as it takes. If closing a gap is a year, we say a year. Optimistic
timelines are the most damaging thing a career platform can produce — a person reorganizes
their life around them.

Estimates state their basis (typical time-to-competence from learning-resource data and
recorded outcomes) and their confidence. With enough outcomes, estimates become observed
rather than assumed. Until then, they are labeled as assumptions.

## Fairness constraints

- **No proxy discrimination.** Never score on nationality, age, gender, name, or institution
  prestige. Location matters only where it is a genuine legal or market constraint — and then
  it is named as such.
- **Non-linear paths are normal.** Career breaks, self-taught backgrounds, and
  non-traditional routes are not penalized; they change which evidence we look for.
- **Language is a real constraint, stated plainly.** Not a deficiency — a requirement of a
  specific market, with the level actually needed.
- **The user sees what the model believes about them, and can correct it.** A profile is
  editable, and corrections carry more weight than inferences.

## Anti-patterns in scoring

- A single opaque number with no components.
- Percentage-of-keywords-matched dressed up as fit.
- Confidence that reflects fluency rather than data quality.
- Encouragement instead of a number ("you're a great fit!" with no gap).
- Penalizing a gap the person could close in a week the same as one taking two years.
